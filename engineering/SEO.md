# SEO

- **HTML Metadata**
  > Provide Google with information that is not visible to users, such as the page title, the description that may appear in search results,
  the canonical version of a page when multiple URLs exist, the supported languages, and whether a page should be indexed or ignored.

  - Guides
  - Tools

- **Semantic HTML**
  > Helps Google distinguish the primary content from supporting content. It allows Google to identify the main content of the page,
  navigation areas, content sections, the hierarchy of headings, and the meaning of images and links, resulting in a more accurate understanding of the page structure.

  - Guides
  - Tools

- **Technical SEO**
  > Ensures that Google can discover, crawl, render, and index pages correctly. It helps Google find all pages on a website, identify the preferred URLs,
  understand when pages have moved or been removed, and avoid indexing duplicate content.

  - Guides
  - Tools

- **Search Engine Preferences**
  > Recommendations and best practices by search engines that are not required for indexing, but help search engines better understand, trust, and rank websites.

  - Guides
  - Tools

- **Structured Data**
  > Provide Google with a structured representation of the page's content. Instead of inferring that a piece of text is an article headline,
  a number is a product price, or a name is an author, structured data explicitly defines these relationships.
  This also allows Google to understand connections between entities (e.g., who wrote an article or which organization published it) and potentially display rich results.

  - Guides
  - Tools
